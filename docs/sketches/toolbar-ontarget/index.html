Studio toolbar sketch — on-target

A verb's control is drawn on its target, or it is not drawn at all. Where no projection draws the target, the verb is legal-and-unrouted, and the utterance says so with the typed reason.

Static HTML and CSS, no script. Scenes are :target; every state below is a radio input and a CSS rule — click the handles and the stops. Source: docs/notes/2026-08-22-studio-toolbar-gen2-opus.md (PR #36). Locus ids, spans, legal sets, and the refusal string are the values examples/gameplay-commentary/main.vel and the duplicate-overlay fixture actually produce. This is a sketch: it changes no Studio, Engine, Core, or VEL code.

1The session strip, with the bank gone

Six locus-taking controls have left the actions row, and nothing verb-shaped replaced them here, because a standing control cannot name a locus it did not draw. What is left passes a membership test rather than sitting on a list. Click any control in the strip to read it against the test.

Membership testreading
rule A control may stand permanently only if it commits no SemanticEdit and its subject is the session, the document, or the application — something that is always present. Stated as the thing it is really about: no standing control may name a locus it did not draw.
open Membership is not settled here. The rows below are the current strip classified against the test, as an input rather than a decision.
stands Play, Pause, Seek, Scrub commit no SemanticEdit, and their subject is the transport, which is always present. They name no locus.
stands Zoom In and Zoom Out commit no SemanticEdit; the subject is the viewport. This pair is also the affordance that restores a verb reported as too-small-to-draw.
stands Save, Resolve, Open Video… commit no SemanticEdit; the subject is the document or a phase. Resolve is the Resolve phase, not an edit to a locus.
stands CPU / GPU DX12 and the two status readings commit no SemanticEdit; the subject is the explicit renderer request and the device state. A GPU request still either selects DX12 or returns a typed error.
argued Undo and Redo mutate source, so they fail the test as first written. They pass the rule the test is for: their subject is the volatile session history, which is always present, and they take no LocusId, so they cannot invent a target or retarget anything.
conditional Copy locus JSON commits no SemanticEdit, but it reads on here, so it can be pressed when here is unset. It stands only if it fails closed and says what is missing instead of copying something plausible.
The strip is not the utterance. It carries no clause, and no clause carries a control.

●Why refusals still exist, and why a pointer cannot reach one

A handle is drawn only where the Engine can name a target, so ordinary pointing cannot produce "X is not legal for here". That is the cheap falsifier for the whole rule: after the change, any path that yields a refusal from a pointer alone is a surviving bank.

refuse_edit does not disappear. The keyboard, an agent, and the CLI still arrive without a pointer, and they commit against a LocusId directly — routing is a Studio fact and does not bind them. Accelerators are the narrow case: one is live exactly while its handle is drawn, and commits exactly what that handle commits, so the bank cannot regrow invisibly in the keymap.

5Overlap still resolves through Projection-Local

Handles change nothing about pointing. An ambiguous point still holds itself open on the projection that was touched and lists its candidates, and here stays unset until a card is picked. The dense-tie fixture is the stress test: two titles and a callout with identical text "Same" and identical spans 0s–2s, carrying distinct LocusIds. Pick a card, and switch the touched projection.

●Local routing, global legality

Switching the touched projection changes which verbs that projection commits. It does not change the candidate list, the legal set, or which locus is here. Legality stays global and Engine-owned and only drawing and routing are local, so a verb with no drawn handle anywhere is still legal and is still spoken.

Sketch note. This scene reopens nothing. The candidate list, the held-open point, and the one-locus rule are exactly as they are on main; the only change is that after a pick, the verbs the picked locus routes are drawn on it instead of standing in a row.

Locks this sketch is drawn inside

  • Overlap resolves through Projection-Local's candidate list on the projection that was touched — scene 5.
  • A click on a video clip points the source clip and keeps its identity, with no promotion to the containing scene — scene 4.
  • One locus, one Engine legal set, one utterance — every scene shows exactly one here and one reading.
  • No silent retarget: a control never rewrites a target while the displayed locus stays put — scenes 3 and 4 speak the relation instead.
  • Title-shaped Inspector fields appear only when here is Title — scenes 2 and 3 show the Inspector saying so.
  • No per-view selection — the audio and video blocks resolve to one LocusId, and every view reads it.
  • The utterance is disclosure-only. It may carry Navigate and explicit seek: it may move the eye, and it may never move the source.
